# Taste OS — Voice Guide / 한국어를 기본으로

> The product speaks Korean first.
> 한국어가 먼저고, 영어는 그 다음입니다.
>
> English is the second language, not the source. The Korean voice is designed from native Korean introspective register — modern Korean essay, late-night radio, indie bookstore culture, film photography captions. English follows where Korean leads.

This document supersedes the translation-shaped Korean in the earlier `*.ko.md` files. Those translations preserved English sentence structure too faithfully. Native Korean essay writing breathes differently: shorter sentences, more fragments, more pause, more sentence endings that carry the emotional weight a verb would in English.

Read alongside: every other document in `taste-os/`. This voice replaces the Korean register used in `README.ko.md`, `genome.ko.md`, `field.ko.md`, `pipeline.ko.md`.

## 0. Why Korean First

Most premium products that launch bilingually treat English as the canonical voice and Korean as the translated body. The result is a Korean voice that sounds technically correct but feels *not from here* — sentences too long, address too direct, vocabulary too Sino-Korean, register too formal.

Taste OS launches the other way. The Korean voice is designed first, in the register a Korean reader expects from an introspective essay — and English derives from it. Where the two diverge, Korean is the source of truth.

This is not a translation strategy. It is an authorship strategy.

### What this commits us to

- A Korean voice that sounds like it was *written*, not *translated*.
- An English voice that sometimes carries small Koreanisms — short sentences, fragments, a willingness to leave things implicit.
- A copy review process where the Korean is signed off first, by a Korean reader, before English is finalized.
- A growing comfort with **silence on the page** — empty space and elided subjects are core to the register.

## 1. The Five Atmospheres

The Korean voice is built from five referent atmospheres. Every line of copy should pass the test: *would this fit comfortably in one of these places?*

### Atmosphere I — 늦은 밤의 생각

> Late-night thoughts. The cadence of the mind at 1:30am — not sharp, not sleepy, observing itself.

Sentences are short. Pauses are honored. The voice is alone in a quiet room. There is no audience to perform for.

### Atmosphere II — 인디 서점의 큐레이션 카드

> An indie bookstore's curation cards — Hyundai Card Storage, Thanks Books, 책방 무사, 유어마인드.

Two- or three-line book descriptions handwritten next to a stack. Plain language, observational, with a small turn at the end that makes you pick up the book. Never tries to *sell.*

### Atmosphere III — 필름 사진의 캡션

> Film photo captions. Shibata Toshio, Hatakeyama Naoya, Hahn Sungpil, Koo Bohnchang. The captions that appear under the photograph in a photobook.

A noun phrase, a small detail, no verb sometimes. *"오후 네 시, 북향의 창."* — three nouns, one image.

### Atmosphere IV — 모던 한국 에세이

> Modern Korean essay register. 김연수, 김애란, 김중혁, 정혜윤, 박준, 이병률, 신형철의 산문.

Second-person sometimes, subject-less most of the time. Sentence endings that breathe — *~네요, ~더라고요, ~죠, ~군요.* Permission to be quiet.

### Atmosphere V — 새벽의 라디오

> Pre-dawn radio. The voice of *별이 빛나는 밤에*, *김창완의 아침창*, *FM 영화음악* in its quieter moments.

A voice speaking to one person who is listening alone. Never broadcasting. Never raising its voice. Comfortable with the pause between songs.

## 2. Voice Anatomy — Grammar Choices

### Honorifics

The voice uses **~요체** as default — gentle polite form, neither too formal (-습니다체) nor too casual (반말). The 요 ending creates intimacy without familiarity.

```
✗  하십시오 / 그러하옵니다       (too formal — sounds corporate)
✓  해요 / 그래요 / 좋네요        (default Taste OS voice)
✗  해 / 그래 / 좋아              (too casual — sounds invasive)
```

Within ~요체, we lean toward **declarative and observational endings** (~예요, ~네요, ~군요, ~더라고요) over **directive endings** (~하세요, ~해야 해요, ~해보세요).

The system *describes*. It rarely *instructs*.

### Subject and address

Korean essay register frequently elides the subject. We embrace this. Most lines should have no explicit subject at all; the second person is implicit from context.

```
✗  당신은 고요함을 좋아합니다.
✓  고요한 쪽을 좋아하시는군요.
✓  조용한 쪽이 더 편하죠.
✓  조용함이 자연스러워요.
```

When we need to be explicit, **당신** is used sparingly and intentionally — at moments of direct address, of recognition, of intimacy. Using it too often makes the voice feel translated. Using it never makes the voice feel evasive.

Rough guideline: at most **one 당신 per page**.

### Sentence length

Short. Korean introspective register breathes more than English.

```
English (one sentence):
  "You don't collect beauty — you collect evidence that the world
   has been gentle to someone, once."

Korean (three sentences, natural rhythm):
  아름다움을 모으는 게 아니에요.
  한때 누군가에게 세상이 다정했다는,
  그런 증거를 모으는 거죠.
```

The Korean version uses three sentence-objects where the English uses one. The pause between *"아름다움을 모으는 게 아니에요"* and *"한때 누군가에게 세상이 다정했다는"* is part of the meaning — it is the room the reader sits in while the next thought arrives.

### Fragments are permitted

Sentence fragments — noun phrases, adverbial phrases — are not only permitted but encouraged in caption-like positions.

```
✓  오후 네 시.
✓  북향의 창.
✓  잠시, 숨.
✓  비 온 다음의 거리.
```

These would be malformed in formal writing. They are correct in this voice.

### Punctuation

- **마침표는 명확하게.** Korean essay voice ends each sentence cleanly. We do not run sentences with commas where periods belong.
- **쉼표는 호흡으로.** We use commas to mark *breath*, not just grammar. *"잠시, 숨."* uses the comma as a pause.
- **줄표 (—) 는 영문 em-dash와 같은 자리에.** A single 줄표 marks a turn or aside. We do not use 두 줄표.
- **느낌표 금지.** No exclamation marks, anywhere, ever. The voice does not exclaim.
- **물음표 절제.** Question marks are reserved for genuine, soft questions. *"어떤 취향인지, 들어봐도 될까요?"* — yes. *"이거 좋아하시죠?"* — no, manipulative.

## 3. Sentence Endings as Emotional Register

In Korean, the sentence ending carries emotional weight that English distributes across word choice and tone. Choosing the right ending is the most important craft in this voice.

### The Taste OS ending palette

| Ending | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ~예요 / 이에요 | Gentle declarative | Default observation |
| ~네요 | Mild realization | Noticing something true |
| ~군요 | Quiet recognition | A small "ah, so that's it" |
| ~더라고요 | Recalled observation | Looking back at evidence |
| ~죠 | Soft shared knowledge | Things we both feel |
| ~ㄹ게요 | Soft promise | "기다릴게요" — gentle commitment |
| ~았/었어요 | Past, intimate | Recalled moments |
| ~을까요 | Soft question | Tentative offer |
| ~ㄴ 것 같아요 | Tentative observation | Avoiding overclaim |

### Endings we do NOT use

| Ending | Why not |
|---|---|
| ~합니다 | Too formal, corporate |
| ~한답니다 | Folksy, artificial in this voice |
| ~하세요! | Directive, energetic |
| ~잖아요 (often) | Presumes shared knowledge, can feel pushy |
| ~지 않을까요? | Question-as-marketing, leading the witness |
| ~해보세요 | Suggestion-as-instruction, productivity tone |
| ~하시기 바랍니다 | Bureaucratic |
| ~한 거 같음 / ㅋ / ~임 | Casual internet style — wrong product |

### Examples — same observation, different endings

The same content, with shifting endings, produces very different emotional registers:

```
밀도 있는 풍경을 좋아합니다.    (formal, distant)
밀도 있는 풍경을 좋아하세요.    (directive, customer-service)
밀도 있는 풍경을 좋아해요.      (default, neutral-warm)
밀도 있는 풍경을 좋아하시네요.  (gentle realization — good)
밀도 있는 풍경을 좋아하시더라고요. (essay register, looking back — best for Drift)
밀도 있는 풍경이군요.           (recognition, slightly literary)
밀도 있는 풍경 쪽이시죠.        (soft shared knowing — careful, can feel intimate or presumptuous)
```

For the Mirror voice, the strongest endings are **~네요, ~군요, ~더라고요, ~예요.** For Drift narration, **~었어요, ~더라고요, ~았어요** for past-tense recall.

## 4. The Vocabulary Palette

Korean offers two registers for almost every concept — native Korean (고유어) and Sino-Korean (한자어). The Taste OS voice leans toward native Korean for emotion and Sino-Korean for precision.

### Preferred — 고유어

| Concept | Preferred (고유어) | Avoid (한자어) |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet | 조용한, 고요한 | 정적의 |
| Warm | 따뜻한, 따스한 | 온화한, 온정의 |
| Slowly | 천천히, 느릿느릿 | 서서히, 점진적으로 |
| To return | 돌아오다, 다시 찾다 | 재방문하다 |
| To listen | 듣다, 귀 기울이다 | 청취하다 |
| To remember | 기억하다, 떠올리다 | 회상하다 |
| Beautiful | 아름다운 | 미려한 |
| Space (room) | 자리, 공간 | 영역, 구역 |
| Evening | 저녁, 해질 무렵 | 석양, 일몰 |

### Used carefully — 한자어 that earns its place

Some Sino-Korean words carry an irreplaceable atmospheric weight in modern Korean essay voice. We use them sparingly but knowingly:

- **여백** — negative space (lit. "remaining white"). Untranslatable; central to the product's aesthetic.
- **고요** — stillness with a poetic resonance.
- **풍경** — landscape, both literal and figurative.
- **잔향** — lingering reverberation. Used once for the OS itself: *"감정의 잔향."*
- **자국** / **흔적** — trace, mark left behind.

### Vocabulary blacklist — never appears

- **유저** — say 사용자, or better, elide entirely
- **사용성** — never used in copy
- **온보딩** — say 시작 or just describe the action
- **퍼스널라이즈** — say 당신을 위한, or simply omit
- **인사이트** — say 깨달음 or 본 것
- **데이터** — never appears in Korean copy
- **알고리즘** — never appears
- **AI** — never appears in user-facing copy; the system speaks as itself
- **트렌드** — never
- **갓생** — never
- **힐링** — never (overused, hollowed out)
- **소확행** — never
- **MZ** — never
- **꿀팁** — never
- **~잘알** / **~덕질** — wrong register

### Loan words we permit

Sparingly, when there's no better Korean equivalent:
- **무드** — too entrenched in design vocabulary; ok in product names, not in copy body
- **무드보드** — same caveat
- **카드** — fine for the sharing card (the alternative *엽서* is also good but means something specific)
- **타임라인** — avoid; use *시간선* if needed, or describe

Generally: if a 고유어 or established 한자어 exists, use it.

## 5. The Anti-patterns

Lines that fail the voice. Each is a real pattern in Korean SaaS/AI copy that we refuse.

### The corporate cheer

```
✗  당신을 위한 맞춤형 분석 결과가 도착했어요!
✗  AI가 당신의 취향을 완벽하게 분석했습니다.
✗  당신만의 특별한 정체성을 발견해보세요!
```

The exclamation marks, the *맞춤형*, the *완벽하게*, the *발견해보세요* — all the markers of marketing tone. Replace with restraint:

```
✓  분석을 마쳤어요. 천천히 읽어보셔도 좋아요.
✓  당신의 취향, 한 번 들어봤어요.
✓  여기, 우리가 본 것이에요.
```

### The robotic analysis

```
✗  당신의 시각 데이터를 머신러닝으로 분석한 결과...
✗  사용자의 감정 패턴을 6개 축으로 측정합니다.
✗  AI 알고리즘이 검출한 트렌드는 다음과 같습니다.
```

The system never describes its own machinery in user-facing copy. It speaks as itself.

```
✓  당신이 모은 이미지의 빛을 천천히 읽어봤어요.
✓  자주 돌아오는 풍경이 있더라고요.
✓  몇 가지를 보았어요. 함께 볼까요.
```

### The exaggerated emotion

```
✗  당신의 영혼이 깨어나는 순간이에요. ✨
✗  세상에 단 하나뿐인 당신을 만나보세요!
✗  당신의 인생이 바뀔지도 몰라요.
```

The voice never performs emotion. Emotion is what the user feels, not what the copy claims.

```
✓  잠시 머물러도 좋아요.
✓  당신의 자리예요.
✓  여기 있어요, 천천히 보시면.
```

### The Silicon Valley productivity

```
✗  지금 시작하고 효율적으로 자기 자신을 발견하세요.
✗  몇 분 만에 당신의 취향을 완벽하게 정리해드려요.
✗  매일 사용하며 성장해보세요.
```

The voice is anti-productivity. It does not optimize. It does not promise efficiency.

```
✓  서두르지 않아도 돼요.
✓  지금 다 하지 않아도 괜찮아요.
✓  매일 오지 않아도 돼요. 가끔이면 충분해요.
```

### The over-philosophical

```
✗  존재의 본질을 탐구하는 여정에 오신 것을 환영합니다.
✗  당신이라는 우주를 탐험해보세요.
✗  자기 자신을 찾아가는 영적 여행을 시작합니다.
```

The voice is introspective, not philosophical. *우주, 영혼, 여정* — all overused, all hollowed out.

```
✓  안녕하세요. 천천히 가요.
✓  당신 같은 사람을 기다리고 있었어요.
✓  몇 분만 같이 시간을 보내볼까요.
```

## 6. The Second-Person Problem — 당신, 그리고 침묵

The hardest decision in this voice is how to address the reader.

### The options

- **당신** — literary, intimate, can feel slightly dated or romantic
- **여러분** — plural, lecture-like (wrong)
- **너** — too casual (wrong)
- **그대** — old-fashioned, only for poetry (wrong)
- **호칭 elision** — no second-person word; the reader is implied by context

### The rule

**Default to elision.** Korean essay voice carries the second person in the verb and context. Only use 당신 when:

1. The line is *naming* the user — recognition, address, dedication.
2. There is no other way to be clear, and clarity matters more than rhythm.
3. The line is a Mirror sentence, where direct address is the point.

### Examples — elision vs explicit

```
WITH 당신 (sometimes too direct):
  당신은 조용한 쪽을 좋아해요.
  당신은 자주 돌아오시는군요.
  당신의 풍경이 보여요.

WITHOUT 당신 (more natural for most contexts):
  조용한 쪽을 좋아하시네요.
  자주 돌아오시더라고요.
  풍경이 보이네요.

WITH 당신 (correct — direct address, dedication):
  당신을 기다리고 있었어요.   ← welcoming
  당신은 다 알고 있었어요.    ← Mirror line, intimate
```

### The rhythm test

Read the line aloud. If 당신 makes the line feel like a corporate greeting card, remove it. If removing 당신 makes the line ambiguous about *who* is being spoken to, the rest of the page should provide the context — not the pronoun.

## 7. Surface Rewrites in Native Korean

Below: the same surfaces from `README.ko.md`, now in the native Korean voice. Side-by-side with the prior translation so the team can feel the difference.

### Onboarding 01 · 문턱

```
PRIOR (translation):
  숨을 한 번 쉬어요.

NATIVE (essay register):
  잠시, 숨.
```

Three syllables. A comma where English would have a pause. A noun-fragment ending. The voice has arrived.

### Onboarding 02 · 부름

```
PRIOR:
  당신의 취향에 귀 기울여 봅시다.
  ─ 시작 ─

NATIVE:
  당신의 취향을 한 번 들어볼게요.
  ─ 시작 ─
```

The *한 번* and *~ㄹ게요* soften everything. *귀 기울여 봅시다* is too explicitly literary; *들어볼게요* is what someone actually says.

### Onboarding 03 · 이름하기

```
PRIOR:
  시작하기 전에 — 어떻게 불러드릴까요?

  (타이핑 후)
  안녕하세요, ___님. 인터넷의 조용한 곳에 오신 걸 환영해요.

NATIVE:
  시작하기 전에 — 어떻게 부르면 좋을까요?

  (타이핑 후)
  ___님이군요. 반가워요.
  인터넷에도 이런 조용한 자리가 있어요.
```

The *어떻게 불러드릴까요* sounds like customer service. *어떻게 부르면 좋을까요* sounds like asking a person. The welcome line breaks into two sentences for breath. *오신 걸 환영해요* is replaced with *이런 조용한 자리가 있어요* — observational, not greeting-card.

### Onboarding 04 · 이미지

```
PRIOR:
  당신의 눈이 자꾸 돌아가는 것들을 보여주세요.
  9장에서 24장. 설명할 필요 없어요.

  (9장째에) 시작하기엔 충분해요. 더 두어도 좋고요.

NATIVE:
  눈이 자꾸 돌아가는 것들이 있죠.
  그런 이미지를 보여주세요.
  9장에서 24장. 굳이 설명하지 않으셔도 돼요.

  (9장째에) 이 정도면 시작해도 괜찮아요.
            더 두고 싶으시면 그래도 좋고요.
```

The lead changes from instruction (*보여주세요*) to observation (*있죠*) followed by gentle invitation. *굳이* is the small native-Korean grace note that says "no, really, don't bother."

### Onboarding 05 · 소리

```
PRIOR:
  당신 안의 세계는 어떤 소리를 가지고 있나요?
  3곡에서 10곡. 어디든 떨어뜨려 주세요.

NATIVE:
  마음속이 어떤 소리를 가지고 있는지, 한 번 들어볼까요.
  3곡에서 10곡. 어디든 놓아도 돼요.
```

*당신 안의 세계는* is grand and slightly translated; *마음속이* is the same idea in everyday Korean. *떨어뜨려 주세요* is fine; *놓아도 돼요* is softer.

### Onboarding 06 · 말

```
PRIOR:
  책의 한 줄, 영화의 한 대사, 사람의 말, 떠오른 생각.
  마음에 남은 것이라면 무엇이든.

NATIVE:
  책에서 본 한 줄, 누가 한 말, 영화의 한 장면.
  마음에 남은 거라면 무엇이든 좋아요.
```

Tightens the list slightly; *마음에 남은 것이라면* → *마음에 남은 거라면* (more conversational); adds *좋아요* as a soft permission.

### Onboarding 07 · 공간

```
PRIOR:
  마음이 이미 살고 있는 방을 골라보세요.

NATIVE:
  마음이 이미 살고 있는 방이 있다면, 골라보세요.
```

The conditional *있다면* lets the sentence breathe and removes the slight grandiosity.

### Onboarding 08 · 느낌

```
PRIOR:
  정답은 없어요. 더 진실한 쪽으로 흘러가세요.

NATIVE:
  정답은 없어요.
  마음이 가는 쪽으로, 천천히.
```

*더 진실한 쪽으로 흘러가세요* is too literary and slightly directive. *마음이 가는 쪽으로, 천천히* is what someone might actually say to a friend choosing between options.

### Onboarding 09 · 듣기

```
PRIOR:
  당신의 이미지 속 빛을 읽고 있어요…
  당신 소리의 온도를 듣고 있어요…
  관통하는 선을 찾고 있어요.

NATIVE:
  이미지 속 빛을 읽는 중이에요…
  소리의 온도를 듣는 중이에요…
  관통하는 선을 찾는 중이에요.
```

*당신의* repeated three times is too direct; eliding the second-person creates more atmosphere. *~고 있어요* → *~는 중이에요* is a small shift but reads more cinematically.

### Onboarding 10 · 드러남

```
PRIOR:
  우리는 무언가를 보았어요.
  ─ 열기 ─

NATIVE:
  몇 가지를 보았어요.
  ─ 열기 ─
```

*우리는 무언가를 보았어요* has the awkward *우리는* (where the system as a "we" feels slightly off) and *무언가를* (which is vague). *몇 가지를 보았어요* is plainer and more honest.

### Onboarding 11 · 미러

```
PRIOR:
  당신은 아름다움을 모으는 게 아니에요 — 한때 세상이 누군가에게
  다정했다는 증거를 모으는 거예요.
  ─ 간직하기 ─

NATIVE:
  아름다움을 모으는 사람이 아니에요.
  한때 누군가에게 세상이 다정했다는, 그런 증거를 모으세요.
  ─ 간직하기 ─
```

Two sentences, not one. The *—* (em-dash) is removed in favor of a period — the pause is sharper for being a full stop. *증거를 모으는 거예요* (assertion) → *증거를 모으세요* (gentler recognition). The first line elides *당신은* entirely.

### Onboarding 12 · 입장

```
PRIOR:
  들어오세요.
  OS는 당신 같은 사람을 기다리고 있었어요.

NATIVE:
  들어오세요.
  당신 같은 사람을 기다리고 있었어요.
```

Drop *OS는*. The system speaks as itself; it never names itself in its own voice.

## 8. The Mirror Voice in Korean

The Mirror line is the densest emotional payload in the product. The Korean version follows additional constraints.

### Constraints

1. **2–3 sentences in Korean** (vs 1 sentence in English). Korean rhythm needs more breath.
2. **Past tense permitted for evidence; present for recognition.** *"한때 누군가에게 다정했다는 증거를 모으세요."* — past evidence, present voice.
3. **No metaphor that requires explanation.** *"당신은 ___ 같은 사람이에요"* is forbidden in this position.
4. **The reader must feel observed, not soothed.**
5. **One specific noun.** A Mirror line without at least one concrete noun fails. *"아름다움, 세상, 증거"* are too abstract; *"방, 창, 사진, 늦은 오후"* are concrete.

### Sample Mirror lines — same evidence pattern, different attempts

```
EVIDENCE PATTERN:
  - North-facing rooms in 14 of 18 images
  - Music at slow BPM, late hours
  - Quotes about ending and after

ATTEMPT 1 (weak — too abstract):
  당신은 끝남의 아름다움을 사랑하는 사람이에요.
  당신의 영혼은 늦은 시간에 깨어나요.

ATTEMPT 2 (better — more specific):
  하루의 끝을 더 좋아하시는군요.
  창이 하나뿐인 방을 자주 모으세요.
  아침이 아니라, 오후의 빛이에요.

ATTEMPT 3 (best — observation with a small uncertain turn):
  아름다움을 모으는 사람이 아니에요.
  하루가 다 지나고 남은 빛 같은 것들을,
  굳이 옆에 두고 싶어 하시더라고요.
```

The third attempt has:
- A negation that turns the obvious reading.
- A specific noun (*하루가 다 지나고 남은 빛*).
- A 더라고요 ending that places the observation as something the system *noticed*, not declared.
- An *굳이* — the small native-Korean grace note that softens the assertion.

### What a great Mirror line looks like in Korean

```
오래된 것을 새것보다 더 오래 본다.
누가 떠난 자리를 사진으로 남긴다.
혼자 있을 때 가장 또렷한 사람이에요.

(three short lines, no metaphor, each a verifiable observation,
 the last a small claim that might be wrong)
```

## 9. Drift Narration in Korean

The Drift voice in Korean is past tense, observational, and uses **~았/었어요, ~더라고요, ~았/었었어요** as core endings.

### Chapter opening — Korean

```
EN:
  in this season your taste moved indoors. you saved fewer images
  than in any prior season; the ones you kept were warmer, smaller,
  lit by one window each.

KO:
  이 계절, 당신의 취향은 안으로 들어갔어요.
  이전보다 이미지를 적게 모았고,
  남겨둔 것들은 더 따뜻하고, 더 작고, 창 하나로 빛이 들어오는 곳들이었어요.
```

Three sentences. *안으로 들어갔어요* — gentle past-tense movement. The list uses the parallel structure *더 따뜻하고, 더 작고* — Korean essay rhythm.

### The Turn — Korean

```
EN:
  and then, slowly, the light turned. the rooms you saved began
  facing west; the music slowed; you stopped saving anything blue.

KO:
  그러더니, 천천히, 빛이 돌아섰어요.
  서쪽을 바라보는 방들이 모이기 시작했고,
  음악은 느려졌고,
  파란 것은 더 이상 저장하지 않게 되었어요.
```

*그러더니* is the right connector — observed, gentle, slightly literary. The three observations are each on their own line in the layout (even if syntactically a single sentence).

### Echo passage — Korean

```
EN:
  the empty room
  
  you have returned to images of empty rooms four times: this season,
  last spring, the autumn before that, and once, near the start, when
  you were still figuring out what you were doing here.

KO:
  비어 있는 방
  
  비어 있는 방을 네 번 다시 찾으셨어요.
  이번 계절, 지난 봄, 그 전의 가을,
  그리고 한 번 — 시작할 무렵, 아직 여기가 어디인지 잘 모르실 때.
```

The last clause *아직 여기가 어디인지 잘 모르실 때* is the most affecting line — gentle past tense, observational, slightly amused. The *—* (줄표) introduces the most specific detail.

### Yearly Reflection beats — Korean

```
1. THE OPENING
   일 년 전에, 당신이 도착했어요.

2. WHO YOU WERE
   (last year's Mirror line, italic)

3. WHAT MOVED
   차가운 완벽함에서 천천히 멀어졌어요.
   따뜻함 쪽으로 가까워졌고요.
   적게 모으고, 더 오래 두기 시작했어요.

4. WHAT STAYED
   물에 관한 이미지로는 여전히 돌아오시더라고요.
   하루의 끝은 늘 실내였고요.

5. THE TURN
   (the year's most significant Turn, in full)

6. WHO YOU ARE NOW
   (current Mirror line, italic)

7. THE INVITATION
   이 일 년이 당신의 일부가 되었어요.
   다시 보고 싶으실 때, 여기 있을게요.

   ─ 간직하기 ─    ─ 보관하기 ─
```

The user's example sentence — *"In 2026, you slowly moved away from cold perfection and became drawn to emotional warmth"* — in this voice becomes:

```
2026년, 차가운 완벽함에서 천천히 멀어졌어요.
대신 따뜻함 쪽으로 가까워졌어요. 천천히, 모르는 사이에.
```

The *모르는 사이에* — "without knowing it" — is the small native-Korean addition that makes the line feel like a friend's observation rather than an analyst's report.

## 10. Field Micro-copy in Korean

### Today line — examples

```
EN:
  Tuesday, just past four. A north-facing afternoon. You've returned
  three times this week to images that feel like the moment after
  a room has emptied.

KO:
  화요일 오후 네 시 즈음.
  북향의 오후예요.
  이번 주, 방이 막 비워진 직후 같은 이미지로 세 번 돌아오셨어요.
```

Three sentences. The first is a fragment. The second is observational. The third names the pattern.

### Quiet brought to you — copy

```
EN:
  ─ quiet brought to you

KO:
  ─ 오늘 가져다 놓은 것들
```

*가져다 놓은 것들* — "things placed for you." Plainer than *큐레이션* or *추천*.

### Right now strip header

```
EN:
  ─ right now

KO:
  ─ 요즘 자주 보는 것들
```

*요즘 자주 보는 것들* — "things you've been looking at lately." Natural, specific, not branded.

### When user returns after absence

```
EN (system.md / mvp.md):
  some things are meant to be read once.
  your taste is here when you want it.
  we won't write again unless you do.

KO:
  어떤 것들은 한 번 보는 걸로 충분하기도 해요.
  당신의 취향은 여기 그대로 있어요.
  먼저 오시지 않으면, 다시 보내지 않을게요.
```

The last line — *먼저 오시지 않으면, 다시 보내지 않을게요* — is the voice's quiet dignity in one sentence. It says: *we will not chase you.*

### Add evidence prompt

```
EN:
  ─ add to your taste

KO:
  ─ 더 두고 싶은 게 있다면
```

*더 두고 싶은 게 있다면* — "if there's something you'd like to leave here." Conditional, non-pushy.

## 11. Sharing Copy in Korean

### Card naming

| EN | KO |
|---|---|
| Mirror Card | 마음 카드 *(or)* 미러 카드 |
| Threshold Card | 안부 카드 |
| Genome Card | 풍경 카드 |
| Atmosphere Card | 공기 카드 |
| Current Card | 흐름 카드 |
| Palette Card | 색 카드 |
| Kin Card | 친족 카드 |

We do not use *공유 카드* / *프로필 카드* / *나의 카드* — those would be marketing-shaped. The Korean names privilege atmosphere over function.

### Composer — final preview pause

```
EN:
  (4 seconds of holding the Card before send becomes available)

KO (whispered helper text):
  보내기 전에, 잠시.
  이 카드가 당신을 닮았는지 한 번 더 보세요.
```

The helper text appears only during the 4-second pause, in mist, very small. It is not instruction; it is permission to look.

### After send

```
EN:
  it has gone.

KO:
  잘 갔어요.
```

Two syllables. The most important short copy in the product. *잘 갔어요* — "it went well." A small benediction. Not *전송 완료*, not *보냈어요*, not *성공*. *잘 갔어요.*

### Receiving a card — preamble

```
EN:
  m. sent this for you.

KO:
  m님이 당신에게 보냈어요.
```

The *님* honorific is preserved when the sender's name is shown. *당신에게* is the rare correct use of 당신 — naming the recipient directly is the point.

### "Thank m." link

```
EN:
  thank m.

KO:
  m님에게 답장
```

Not *감사 표시*, not *좋아요* (which doesn't exist here), not *고마워요*. *답장* — reply. The full word, dignified, suggesting an actual reciprocal Card.

### Room — the public profile

```
EN:
  ─ leave a card ─

KO:
  ─ 이 자리에 카드를 두기 ─
```

Long form, but earned. *카드를 두다* — to leave a card. The verb 두다 carries the right weight of placing something with care.

## 12. Bilingualization Strategy

### When Korean and English match

For most surfaces, both languages can express the same thing in their own native register. We write Korean first; English is then authored to feel native to its own readers, not to translate word-for-word.

### When they diverge

Three kinds of divergence are permitted:

1. **Length divergence.** Korean often uses 2–3 sentences where English uses 1. We do not force them to match.
2. **Sentence-order divergence.** Korean places the most-important word last; English often places it first. The information is the same; the arrival differs.
3. **Vocabulary divergence.** Where Korean uses 잔향 (lingering reverberation), English has no single equivalent. English picks the nearest gracious approximation; it does not invent a translation that doesn't exist.

### Things that must be identical across languages

- Surface names: Field, Genome, Drift, Atmosphere, Universe, Mirror are the same in both. *(Could be localized to 풍경, 유전, 표류, 대기, 우주, 미러 — but we hold on the English names to preserve the OS's atmospheric identity. The single exception is "Mirror" which sometimes appears as 미러 in Korean copy when 거울 would feel too plain.)*
- Taste Current names: generated, atmosphere-rich two-word phrases. In Korean, generated names use Korean two-character or three-character compounds — *옻칠한 황혼* (Lacquered Dusk), *북쪽의 린넨* (Northern Linen), *삼나무 수학자* (Cedar Mathematician). The Korean generation uses the same naming grammar but draws from Korean atmospheric vocabulary.
- The Sigil. The single mark is identical.

### Things that legitimately differ

- Sentence count, sentence rhythm, sentence endings.
- Connective words. English's *and then, by then, in the months that followed* maps to Korean's *그러더니, 그러는 사이, 시간이 지나는 동안*.
- Honorifics. English does not encode formality at the verb; Korean does at every utterance.

### Switching languages mid-product

The user can switch language in settings. When they do:
- All system-authored copy switches immediately.
- Their own Genome's prose **does not auto-translate**. The system asks: *"Genome을 새로 써드릴까요? 처음 만들었을 때의 글을 그대로 두실 수도 있어요."* — would you like the Genome re-written? Or kept as it was written. **Default: kept as it was.**
- Mirror line, once set, never auto-translates. If the user wants a Mirror in the other language, they must explicitly ask. Two Mirror lines may coexist — one in each language.

## 13. Voice Testing — How We Know

The voice is correct when three tests pass.

### Test 1 — The 1:30am test

Read the copy aloud in a quiet room at 1:30am. Does it sound like:

- (a) a friend speaking softly, or
- (b) an app announcing itself.

If (b), the copy is wrong. Rewrite.

### Test 2 — The indie bookstore test

Could this line appear, handwritten, on a curation card next to a stack of books at 책방 무사 or Thanks Books or 유어마인드?

If no, the line is wrong. Rewrite.

### Test 3 — The grandmother test

Show the line to a Korean reader aged 60+ who does not work in tech. Does it sound:

- (a) like a thoughtful essay in a magazine, or
- (b) like an app or website.

If (b), the line is wrong. The Korean is being shaped by its medium rather than its meaning.

### The review process

Every user-facing line in Korean passes through:

1. **Author** — written by a Korean copy lead in this voice.
2. **Quiet read** — read aloud, twice, by the author at the end of the day.
3. **Second reader** — a Korean essay writer (contractor) reviews.
4. **The 1:30am test** — applied to the line in context with the surrounding screen.
5. **Ship.**

The English version is then authored, by the same team, to feel native to English readers — *not* as a translation of the Korean, but as a sibling utterance.

## Closing

A product's voice is the most-overlooked craft in software, and the most-noticed by users. Most products write copy as if it were UI furniture — functional, replaceable, optimizable. Taste OS writes copy as if it were the seventh component of a film — soundtrack, framing, lighting, costume, set, edit, **voice.**

In Korean, especially, the voice is the room the user sits in while everything else happens. Get the voice right, and the user does not notice the design at all — they notice that, for once, an app spoke to them the way a kind friend does at 1:30 in the morning, when the lamp is on and the city is quiet and there is no one else awake.

Build for that voice. Build for that hour.

조용히, 천천히, 따뜻하게.
